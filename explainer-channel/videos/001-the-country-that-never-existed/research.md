# The country that never existed — Gregor MacGregor and the Poyais bonds
- picked: 2026-08-31 · score D4/S5/V5/E5/R5 = 24 · reason: perfect faceless-illustrated subject, timeless con, finance-tier RPM, no dominant recent explainer take

## Facts (script may only use these)
- Gregor MacGregor, a Scot, attempted from 1821 to 1837 to draw British and French investors and settlers to "Poyais", a fictional Central American territory — Britannica / Wikipedia summary
- He claimed King George Frederic Augustus of the Mosquito Coast, Gulf of Honduras, had appointed him "Cazique" (prince) of Poyais — Britannica
- He described Poyais as a developed colony already holding a community of British settlers — Britannica
- Two bond issues on the London market, £200,000 each: first granted October 1822, second October 1823 — Wikipedia summary
- With that money he advertised land lots as large as 540 acres, divisible, at extremely low prices — Wikipedia summary
- He published a guidebook, *Sketch of the Mosquito Shore, including the Territory of Poyais*, printed by William Clowes in 1822 — Britannica
- He staged musical performances of a Poyaisian national anthem and commissioned paintings of the capital city, St Joseph — Britannica
- Settlers were told St Joseph was a European-style capital with a government centre and a theatre — The Hustle / History Skills
- Settlers carried printed Poyaisian currency they had bought with real British money — Britannica / The Hustle
- About 70 settlers left England in autumn 1822 aboard the *Honduras Packet* — The Hustle
- About 180 more sailed from Scotland in January 1823 aboard the *Kennersley Castle* — The Hustle
- They landed at Black River, Honduras, and found no settlement, no harbour, no city — only wilderness — History Skills
- Death toll reported variously: "more than 150 deaths" (Britannica) and "more than 180 settlers had died" by the time rescue completed in 1823 (The Hustle). Script uses the conservative "more than one hundred and fifty".
- Fewer than 50 survivors returned to Britain in late 1823 — Wikipedia summary
- On the news breaking, some victims publicly defended MacGregor, insisting he had been let down by those he put in charge of the emigration party — Wikipedia summary
- A French court tried MacGregor and three others for fraud in 1826 after he ran a variation of the scheme in France; only one associate was convicted — Wikipedia summary
- Between 1822 and 1825 Latin American governments raised roughly £20 million on the London market — NBER / Investor Amnesia
- The Poyais bonds sat inside that boom; Latin American yields surged in autumn 1825 as Poyais and other schemes were exposed — Investor Amnesia
- Perring & Co. paid the interest on the Poyais bonds — Investor Amnesia
- In the Panic of 1825, over 70 banks failed within weeks — Investor Amnesia / Panic of 1825 sources
- The 1825 panic is described as the first modern financial crisis and the birth of the lender of last resort — Market Histories

- Born 24 December 1786 in Stirlingshire, Scotland; died 4 December 1845 in Caracas, Venezuela — DNB / Wikipedia summary
- Joined the republican side in the Venezuelan War of Independence in 1812 and quickly became a general — DNB summary
- Married Josefa, a niece of Simon Bolivar, in 1812 — DNB summary
- 1816: noted retreat from Ocumare to Barcelona with a handful of men before an overwhelming royalist force — DNB summary
- 1817: promoted general of division in the Venezuelan army; received Bolivar's special thanks and the insignia of the Order of Liberators — DNB summary
- 29 June 1817: captured Spanish-held Amelia Island off Florida in a surprise attack, taking the garrison without a shot, and raised the Green Cross flag — Amelia Island Museum / Wikipedia summary
- 1819: captured Puerto Bello, was surprised there and had to flee — DNB summary
